---
name: O.A.S.I.S. presentation principles
description: User requirements for the public-facing O.A.S.I.S. project site.
---

Do not frame O.A.S.I.S. as a student project. Keep the interactive telemetry dashboard on its own page, separate from the presentation landing page. Use readable text and avoid redundant content, generic generated-looking imagery, gratuitous glow/floaters, and orange callout boxes.

For the dashboard, foreground one selected plant at a time. Never reuse another plant's measurements when a plant has no recorded run. Preserve visible, distinct colors for the three primary readings (air temperature, substrate moisture, and VPD), including their values and cues; restraint means no cheap-looking large colored fills, not removing color. Keep other readings secondary. Keep model results, controller proposals, deterministic replay outcomes, human approval, and hardware execution clearly distinct and truthful. Avoid a large enclosing card with many nested boxes and keep dashboard text at readable sizes. Use a bright, botanical, plant-led presentation based on supplied plant imagery; avoid dark futuristic treatments and generic SaaS reskins.

**Why:** the user asked for a readable, low-clutter dashboard with plant-by-plant separation and clear signal identities, then corrected an overcorrection that had made the readings too monochrome. They also repeatedly rejected dark or generic dashboard reskins.

**How to apply:** preserve the landing-page/dashboard separation and the restrained technical presentation when extending this project; treat plant imagery as the visual anchor, keep unavailable plant data unavailable, and label notebook playback as replay.
